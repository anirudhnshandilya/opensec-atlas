\# Governance



\## Purpose



OpenSec Atlas is a community-driven open-source project for building an open and reproducible body of security knowledge around emerging technologies.



The project is intended to remain transparent, collaborative, and independent of any single individual or organization.



\## Maintainers



Maintainers are responsible for:



\- Reviewing contributions

\- Maintaining project quality

\- Protecting project integrity

\- Managing releases

\- Maintaining project infrastructure

\- Resolving technical disputes

\- Supporting contributors



Maintainers should document significant decisions publicly whenever practical.



\## Working Groups



As the community grows, working groups may be established around areas such as:



\- AI Security

\- Emerging Technology

\- Security Research

\- Security Benchmarks

\- Detection Engineering

\- Security Labs

\- Threat Intelligence

\- Community and Documentation



Working groups should have clearly defined scopes and publicly documented responsibilities.



\## Decision Making



Routine technical decisions should happen through issues and pull requests.



Significant architectural, governance, or policy changes should be discussed publicly before adoption.



Technical decisions should prioritize:



1\. Evidence

2\. Reproducibility

3\. Security

4\. Maintainability

5\. Community benefit



\## Community Ownership



OpenSec Atlas should not become dependent on one person.



As the project grows, responsibilities should be distributed across maintainers and contributors.



\## Conflicts of Interest



Contributors and maintainers should disclose relevant conflicts of interest when they materially affect:



\- Security claims

\- Benchmarks

\- Research

\- Technology assessments

\- Recommendations

\- Project decisions



\## Research Integrity



Research contributions should clearly distinguish:



\- Established facts

\- Experimental results

\- Reproduced findings

\- Hypotheses

\- Uncertainty



The project should not present unsupported claims as established security knowledge.



\## Security Research



Security research must respect authorization, responsible disclosure, applicable laws, and affected third parties.



\## Changes to Governance



This document may evolve as OpenSec Atlas grows.



Major governance changes should be proposed publicly and documented through the repository.
